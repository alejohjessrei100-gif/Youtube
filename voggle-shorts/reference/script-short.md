| | reference videos (Pirates / Spy) | short format |

| length | ~160 s, ~545 words | **75–106 s, ~220–300 words** |
| tense | past tense, British, deadpan | **present tense**, plain international English |
| sentences | long build-ups plus short punches | **very short**: many fragments ("Too slow.", "Three down.", "Then land.") |
| characters speak | almost never | **1–4 short quoted lines** per video ("Go home, kid.") |

## Title rules

1. 4–8 words, Title Case, **ends with one emoji** that names the emotion (🤔 😮 🤫 🥹 🤦‍♂️ 😨 💰 🎖️).
2. The title is a **paradox or contradiction** the viewer needs resolved. Five formulas cover all 9:
   - **"How [group/number] [did impossible/forbidden thing]"**: How 22 Orphans…, How A Whole Town…, How He Became…
   - **"Why [crowd] [loves/does X] [shocking object]"**: Why Millions Cheer A Real Killer
   - **"He [verb]ed [kind/odd act] For/To [ironic recipient]"**: He Paid To Feed His Killers, He Planted Flowers For His Blind Wife
   - **"He Built/Made The [thing] That [destroyed him]"**: He Built The Monster That Killed Him
   - **"The [superlative person]'s [opposite superlative]"**: The Smartest Man on Earth's Dumbest Mistake; The World's Youngest Soldier of WWII
3. **Never name the famous person in the title.** Use a role or superlative instead ("Smartest Man on Earth" for Newton).
4. Prefer numbers in the title when the story has a striking one (22 orphans, "Millions").

## Script rules (short format)

1. **Length:** 220–300 words, 75–105 s.
2. **Hook, sentence 1: open on the most dramatic image of the story, in present tense, in the middle of the action.** Often this is a flash-forward to the climax (6 of 9). For example, a boy digging through a bunker, a man dragged toward a dog pit, or a man staring at a stock he sold.
3. **Sentence 2 states the stakes or the paradox** ("If that cut doesn't blister in 10 days, millions could die.").
4. **Rewind** with a time jump: "Months earlier…", "It's 1803.", "For 30 years…". Then tell the story in order.
5. **Short sentences:** mostly 4–12 words, with frequent 1–3-word fragments for beats. Present tense throughout.
6. **Pivots** start sentences: So, / Then, / But / Now / Instead. "Then" ladders build escalation ("…his best friend, then his top adviser, then a noble, then head of the army").
7. **Counting devices** carry the momentum: "Two down, four to go." "Three down." "Day 11."
8. **1–4 lines of character dialogue** in quotes, each under 12 words, at turning points: a dare, a mock, a plea, the reveal. In your voice tool these may need a delivery change.
9. **One light-humour aside** per video, even in serious stories ("The cows don't get a vote.", "Not bad for a guy who used to clean up horse poop.").
10. **Concrete numbers** every 10–15 s (ages, counts, days, money, and "that's millions in today's money").
11. **Name reveal:** famous names appear mid-story for a "wait, it's HIM?" moment (Newton at 0:08), or at the end as the twist (the Oklahoma State mascot).
12. **Climax** is a fast run of fragments (shots, falls, the crowd's reaction).
13. **Kicker: a one-line reframe or moral built on contrast.** For example: "He couldn't fix X, so he gave her Y." "The real gold wasn't in X. It was in Y." "Lost his whole family to the war and found a new one in the middle of it." The callback to the hook image is common.
14. No CTA, no outro, no question at the end.

## Topic range to rotate across 100 videos

Revenge or justice · underdog hero (child, orphan, servant) · love or sacrifice · famous genius's dumb mistake · rags-to-power betrayal · clever trick or loophole · collective or town story · wartime survival · business genius. Eras span ancient to the 1980s. The format gives one clear emotion per video, named by the title emoji.

## Script template (short format, ~90 s, ~260 words)

| beat | time | words | content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Hook image | 0–5 s | 15–25 | The most dramatic moment, present tense, no name |
| 2 Stakes | 5–10 s | 10–20 | What happens if it fails, or the paradox |
| 3 Rewind | 10–25 s | 35–50 | "Months earlier…" or "It's [year]." Who he is, what went wrong |
| 4 Plan or struggle | 25–50 s | 60–80 | The unusual choice, with 1 dialogue line and 1 humour aside |
| 5 Escalation | 50–70 s | 50–60 | Obstacles, using "Then…" ladders or counting devices |
| 6 Climax | 70–85 s | 35–45 | A fast run of fragments, with the dialogue reveal line |
| 7 Kicker | last 5–8 s | 15–25 | A one-line reframe or moral built on contrast, often calling back the hook |
