# Long format script rules (~160 s, reference style)

Derived from the Pirates/Spy reference analysis (analysis/reports/05_story_script.md). Overview in blueprint.md §2.

## Reusable rules

1. **Length:** 520–560 words at 200–210 wpm, which gives 150–165 s. The voice runs continuously and the video ends ≤ 0.3 s after the last word.
2. **Sentences:** 28–33 sentences. Mean 16–19 words, median 16–19, range 5–40. About 15–25 % should be punches of 8 words or fewer, and about 40 % long sentences over 20 words.
3. **Punch placement:** every reversal is a sentence of 8 words or fewer that directly follows a sentence of 15 words or more.
4. **Hook:**
   - Sentence 1 is in medias res: "The [noun/role] [past-tense action]…", with no question and no "you".
   - The protagonist is unnamed in sentence 1.
   - It includes 1 concrete number or place.
   - The first "But" comes at 3–7 s, and the full paradox is stated by 15 s (≤ 10 % of runtime, ≤ 60 words).
5. **Name reveal:**
   - Famous protagonist: withhold the name until the kicker, and refer to them with 3–5 rotating epithets ("the young man", "the hostage", "the young Roman").
   - Obscure protagonist: reveal at 15–20 s with "So [Name], a [mundane former job]…".
6. **Pivots:** one logical pivot (But / So / Instead / Which meant / Until / Then) every 8–10 s, so 16–19 per video. 6–8 "But"s, and 40–50 % of sentences open with And / So / But / Which / Then.
7. **Midpoint turn:** a sentence of 9 words or fewer at 50–55 % of runtime that flips who holds power.
8. **Escalation ladder:** 30–65 s holds at least 3 escalating absurd specifics, and one of them plants a promise or threat that pays off in the final 10 %.
9. **Callbacks:** at least 2 phrase callbacks (exact wording reused), and the kicker or climax must reuse a phrase or a character from the first 20 s.
10. **Third-party complication:** 65–80 % of runtime brings in an authority or third party who blocks, delays or misreads the hero.
11. **Twist payoff:** at 90–96 % of runtime, one more ironic turn, introduced with "..." ("one small mercy...", "...and Hitler signed off on it personally").
12. **Kicker:**
    - One sentence of 9–25 words starting with "And".
    - It is either an identity reveal or a callback, said as an understatement.
    - Full stop, no CTA, no question.
    - Shown on a protagonist close-up reaction.
13. **Specifics:** at least 9 concrete specifics (numbers, places, objects), about 1 every 15 s. Captions spell numbers as words, except years.
14. **Ellipses:** use "..." on screen 3–13 times, only directly before a reveal or cut.
15. **Voice:**
    - Third person, past tense, deadpan British register (British spelling) with heavy contractions.
    - Average word length 4.2–4.4 letters, and at most 4 % of words with 9 or more letters.
    - At least 4 colloquial idioms.
    - No exclamation marks and no hype adjectives.
16. **Irony quota:** at least one of each per script: inversion of expected emotion, dramatic irony (antagonists misjudge him), situational irony, and understatement of something brutal or huge.
17. **Visual coupling:**
    - Every number or object line gets a literal insert.
    - Every movement or geography line gets a parchment map with a red arrow or pin.
    - Every perception or feeling line gets a reaction close-up of the person feeling it.
    - Every abstract joke gets a literalised sight gag.
    - Plan one new image per 8–9 spoken words (about 2.5 s).
18. **Accuracy:** keep the core facts accurate. Compress timelines and drop secondary actors to keep one hero and one causal chain, but never invent the central event.

## Script template (~160 s, ~545 words)

| beat | target time | words | fill-in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Hook | 0–15 s | 55–60 | "The [antagonists/role] [did/demanded X] — [concrete number + tactile conversion]. But instead of [expected reaction], he [opposite reaction]... because [reason]. [6-word inversion punch.]" |
| 2 Setup | 15–33 s | 50–70 | "[Consequence of his audacity]. So, [the hero / 'So [Name], a [mundane job],'] [practical next step]. But that left him [stranded/exposed] [with tiny resources], surrounded by [the most dangerous X]." |
| 3 Escalation ladder | 33–65 s | 100–110 | "He'd be [there/doing this] for [number] [units], and pretty soon, [he] started [acting strangely]... [Absurd detail 1]. [Absurd detail 2, longer]. [Absurd detail 3]. Then, [attitude], he'd [PLANT: promise/threat]." |
| 4 Misread | 65–82 s | 50–60 | "But [antagonists] absolutely [loved it / didn't suspect a thing]. They thought [condescending misjudgement]. So, when [deadline/exchange], they [let him go / paid him], so sure [he was harmless] that [careless detail]." |
| 5 Midpoint turn | 82–100 s | 55–65 | "[≤ 9-word flip: 'But he wasn't laughing anymore.' / 'That was until [Name] made his next move.']. [Fast action sentence with 3 verbs]. By the time [they] realised [truth], it was too late." |
| 6 Complication | 100–122 s | 65–75 | "[Limitation: he had no authority/means], so, he [went to a third party]. But [the third party] didn't see [X]. He saw [Y]. [Third party blocks the hero], which meant [bad outcome]." |
| 7 Climax | 122–145 s | 90–105 | "But [hero] was [emotion] about this, so, he [raced/schemed] [to beat the obstacle]. [Why it works: nobody knew yet]. [Big-number execution sentence: '[number] [units] … for [duration]']." |
| 8 Twist payoff | 145–155 s | 20–60 | "But [softening fact], so he did show them one small [mercy/favour]... [Darker or bigger ironic twist that pays off the beat 3 plant]." |
| 9 Kicker | last 3–7 s | 9–25 | "And that was [Famous Name] being [ironic adjective]." OR "And [time later], [the party who rejected him], who'd [callback], quietly [ironic reward]." |

## Topic-selection checklist (a story must pass at least 7 of 9, and items 1–3 are mandatory)

1. [ ] It is true and documented by at least 2 sources, so the core event is not legend.
2. [ ] One protagonist acts through **audacity, nerve or trickery**, not raw force.
3. [ ] The hook can be written as **one expected reaction that is inverted** in 60 words or fewer.
4. [ ] There are at least 3 absurd, quotable, *drawable* specifics (numbers, objects, quotes).
5. [ ] Power flips at least twice (captive to captor, rejected to indispensable).
6. [ ] There is a promise, threat or plan early that pays off late, so a callback is possible.
7. [ ] The ending can be said as **one ironic line** (reveal, double reward or "being nice").
8. [ ] There is a "wait, that's real?" share trigger: a famous name in a surprising role, or a nobody inside a famous event.
9. [ ] Era and costume are visually distinctive (an ancient, medieval, early-modern or WWI/WWII setting), and the story stays within PG-13 when told deadpan.
