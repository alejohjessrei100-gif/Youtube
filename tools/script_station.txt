A man collapsed in the busiest train station in the city. Hundreds of people walked past. Nobody stopped. One minute. Two. Then a girl, maybe ten, dropped her backpack, pointed straight at a man in a red jacket and shouted, "You! Call an ambulance!" He did. Instantly. You see, when everyone could help, everyone waits for someone else to do it. Ever ignored a question in a group chat because thirty other people could answer it? Psychologists call it the bystander effect. In a famous experiment, people who thought they were alone rushed to help, while people who thought others could hear it often waited. That girl broke it with one finger. So if you ever need help in a crowd, don't shout "somebody." Point at one person.
