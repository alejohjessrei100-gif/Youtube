At ten to five on a Sunday morning, every car in Sweden stops. Millions of people sit still. Nothing moves for ten minutes. Then everyone slowly crosses to the other side of the road. It's 1967, and Sweden is switching from driving on the left to driving on the right. Twelve years earlier, the country voted on it. About eighty-three percent said no. The government did it anyway. Their neighbours all drove on the right, and Swedish cars had the steering wheel on the left. The result surprised everyone. In the months after, accidents fell, because everyone drove carefully. The last car joins the right-hand side at five.
