Every night at 2 AM, the lights came on in Oakwood School. It had been closed for nine years. Neighbors heard chairs scraping, a bell, and voices counting. The police called it a break-in with a very strange burglar. So three officers crept inside with flashlights and kicked open the classroom door. Twelve grown men froze at tiny desks. Truck drivers, a baker, the night janitor. At the board stood old Mrs. Bell, chalk in hand. "Sit down, officer. You're letting the draft in." You see, none of them had ever learned to read, and they were too proud to ask. So she taught them, after dark, where nobody would know. The sergeant took a seat. Now Oakwood is open again, and the class has a waiting list.
