A mapmaker in the 1930s draws a dot on a quiet road in New York State. He labels it Agloe. There is no Agloe. The name is his initials mixed with his partner's. It's a trap. If Agloe ever shows up on someone else's map, they copied his. Years later, it does. On a map by Rand McNally. He's ready to sue. But Rand McNally has an answer. Agloe is real. Someone saw the dot on a map, drove to that empty crossroads, and built a shop there. The Agloe General Store. The fake town on the map made the real store, and the real store made the fake town real. Today the store is gone. The crossroads is empty again.
