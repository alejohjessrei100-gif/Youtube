Six months after the river burst into his tunnel, he throws a dinner party inside it.
He's 21.
Gas candelabra light the tables. Crimson curtains hang in the side arches. A military band in full uniform plays an opera tune.
Over their heads, the Thames keeps flowing.
Two attempts to dig under this river have already failed. Experts said it couldn't be done. All London is laughing, saying the tunnel will never hold water.
So why the dinner? To prove to nervous shareholders that it's safe.
Two months later, the river breaks in again.
Six men die. He's pulled out unconscious.
The work stops for years.
But when the tunnel finally opens in 1843, nearly two million people walk through it in a single year.
And the tunnel London said would never hold water? Trains still run through it today.
