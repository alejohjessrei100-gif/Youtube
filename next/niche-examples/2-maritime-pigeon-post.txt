A reporter ties his story to a pigeon's leg and throws the bird out over the sea.
Ninety kilometres of open water lie between this island and Auckland.
It's 1896.
Two years ago, the steamer Wairarapa wrecked off this coast. A hundred and twenty-one people died. The news took days to reach the mainland.
Back then, the only regular link was a weekly steamer.
Now there's this bird.
She lands in Auckland in under two hours.
Her owner, Walter Fricker, sees what that means.
So he starts a service. His pigeons ride out to the island on the weekly steamer. Then they fly home, up to five messages each, written on tissue paper.
Within months, a rival opens. Both print stamps. Collectors now call them the world's earliest airmail stamps.
Then, in 1908, a telegraph cable is laid across the seabed.
The pigeons stop flying.
