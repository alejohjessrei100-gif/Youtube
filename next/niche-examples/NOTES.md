One example script per niche, written in the structure learned from the reference channel:
in-scene present-tense hook with a puzzle, no date opener, "why" held back, short sentences,
ends on a concrete image, no moral, no invented dialogue, no filler characters.

## Sources and what is solid

### 1. Bramah lock
Wikipedia "Joseph Bramah" and "Alfred Charles Hobbs".
- Challenge lock in the window of 124 Piccadilly from 1790. Sign: "200 guineas the moment it is produced". Solid.
- Hobbs, American, in London for the Great Exhibition of 1851 for Day & Newell, picked Bramah's lock and Chubb's detector lock. 51 hours over 16 days. Awarded the prize after "some argument about the circumstances". Solid.
- Lock is in the Science Museum; rebuilt since Hobbs picked it. Originally 18 iron slides and 1 central spring, now 13 steel slides each with its own spring. Solid.
- FLAG: the Bramah page says the challenge "stood for over 67 years" (that counts from the 1784 patent). From the 1790 display it is 61 years, which is what the script says.
- FLAG: order of the two picks (Chubb vs Bramah) is not in my sources, so the script does not state it.
- Not used because unverified: how Hobbs did it, the exact dispute.

### 2. Pigeon post
Te Papa collection page (primary-quality), plus Smithsonian, Ohinemuri journal and Te Ara in search results.
- 1894 Wairarapa wreck, 121 lives, news took days. 1896 NZ Herald writer, pigeon Ariel, under two hours, owner Walter Fricker. Island is 90 km northeast of Auckland. Weekly steamer was the only regular link. Birds shipped out on the steamer, flew back with up to five messages on tissue paper. Rival agency within months; both issued stamps; "effectively the world's earliest airmail stamps" (unofficial). Service ended 1908 with the underwater telegraph cable. All from Te Papa.
- FLAG: agency start date varies by source (1897 Te Papa, 1898 Ohinemuri). The script avoids the date.
- FLAG: Te Papa says "effectively" and "unofficial"; the script's "Collectors now call them..." is slightly stronger than that. Soften to "Te Papa calls them effectively..." if you want it airtight.
- FLAG: one blog says the Wairarapa wreck started the service; Te Papa only says it highlighted the isolation. The script follows Te Papa.
- "It's 1896" is placed after the hook, not as an opener.

### 3. Ise Shrine
Wikipedia "Ise Grand Shrine" plus a skills-transfer note from several travel and architecture sources.
- Holiest Shinto site; solid cypress, no nails; rebuilt every 20 years on an adjacent site, alternating; 62nd rebuild in 2013, next 2033 on the northern site; empty site (kodenchi) strewn with white pebbles, with a small hut (oi-ya) holding a pole a little over 2 m (shin-no-mihashira); the new shrine is built around that pole; the idea of tokowaka, "forever new and forever ancient"; also described as a way to pass building techniques between generations; 2013 cost 57 billion yen from private donations alone. All from Wikipedia.
- FLAG: the reason for the 20-year cycle is interpretive. Skills transfer and tokowaka are the commonly given reasons; a rice-storage-period theory also exists. The script says "the idea is called tokowaka" and "it's also how the skills pass on", not "this is why".
- FLAG: sources disagree on when it started (about 690) and on gaps in wartime, so the script gives no start date and no "never missed" claim.
- The last line (today's shrine becomes the empty lot in 2033) follows from "alternating sites"; it is an inference, but a direct one.

### 4. Psychology (Beirut replication)
Wikipedia "Realistic conflict theory" and a McKenzie and Twose review.
- Robbers Cave 1954: 22 boys aged 11 to 12 who had never met, researchers posed as camp staff, teams competed and became hostile. Solid.
- Lutfy Diab repeated it with 18 boys from Beirut; groups "Blue Ghost" and "Red Genies", each 5 Christians and 4 Muslims; fighting broke out between Red and Blue, not between Christians and Muslims. From one Wikipedia sentence.
- FLAG (weakest script): the Beirut details rest on one secondary source. The McKenzie and Twose review says Diab abandoned his attempts in Lebanon because hostility got dangerously high, which the Wikipedia sentence does not mention. Need Diab's own paper (about 1970, Genetic Psychology Monographs, from memory, unchecked) before this is produced.
- "But these boys arrive with a label the Oklahoma boys never had" is framing, not a sourced claim about what Diab expected.
- Possible extra beat (also Wikipedia): in two earlier Sherif studies the boys sometimes ganged up on the experimenters themselves. Not used.
- Shortest of the five (103 words); there is not enough verified material for a longer one.

### 5. Mechanical Turk
Wikipedia "Mechanical Turk".
- First shown 1770; life-sized figure in Ottoman robes and turban; cabinet with doors showing clockwork you could see through; sliding seat and dummy machinery to hide the operator; candlelit interior; levers and magnets; toured 84 years; beat most opponents including Napoleon and Franklin; Poe's 1836 essay was famous but many hypotheses were wrong; destroyed by fire in 1854; Silas Mitchell, son of the last private owner, revealed the full secret in The Chess Monthly in 1857, saying there was "no longer any reasons for concealing" it. All from Wikipedia.
- FLAG: Napoleon's game has contradictory accounts (a version where he tries illegal moves is the more plausible); the script only says it beat even Napoleon, not the details.
- FLAG: Wikipedia says the operators during Kempelen's own tour are unknown, so the script does not name an operator.
- Dummy-machinery and seat details are one source (Wikipedia citing Standage and others); a second source would be good before production.
