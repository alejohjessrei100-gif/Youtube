A lock sits in a London shop window. Next to it, a sign.
Pick this lock, and win 200 guineas.
It's 1790. Nobody collects.
Inside: eighteen iron slides and one spring.
Locksmiths try. Sixty-one years go by. The lock stays shut.
Then London hosts the Great Exhibition of 1851. An American locksmith named Alfred Hobbs arrives, with a lock of his own to show.
He sits down in front of Bramah's lock.
It takes him fifty-one hours, spread over sixteen days.
The lock opens.
The firm argues about how he did it. He still gets the 200 guineas.
At the same Exhibition, he picks Chubb's famous detector lock too.
Today, Bramah's lock sits in the Science Museum in London.
It's been rebuilt since. Eighteen iron slides. Now thirteen steel ones.
