Beside the holiest shrine in Japan, there's an empty lot.
White pebbles cover it. Nothing stands here, except one small wooden hut.
Inside is a single pole, just over two metres tall.
The shrine next door is solid cypress, joined without a single nail. It looks perfect.
And in twenty years, it comes down.
The empty lot is where the next one goes. It's built around that pole.
Why destroy a perfect shrine? The idea is called tokowaka: always renewed, so it stays forever new and forever ancient.
It's also how the building skills pass from one generation of carpenters to the next.
In 2013, the sixty-second shrine went up. Fifty-seven billion yen, paid for by private donations alone.
Now the lot beside it waits.
And in 2033, today's shrine becomes the empty lot.
