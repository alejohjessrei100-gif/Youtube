Two armies march toward each other in straight lines, in bright coats, and stop to shoot. Nobody hides. Nobody ducks. It looks insane. So why did European armies fight like this for two hundred years? Start with the gun. A musket's barrel was smooth inside, so the ball left it spinning the wrong way and veered off in a random direction. Past about eighty metres, aiming barely mattered. One soldier on his own could hardly hit anything. But hundreds firing at the same moment could. That was the point of the line: not one shot, but a wall of lead. And it worked, until rifles arrived. Rifled barrels could hit a man hundreds of yards away. Officers kept marching their men in lines anyway.
