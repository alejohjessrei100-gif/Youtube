A king sits down to dinner, and before he takes a single bite, another man eats off his plate. That's not rude. That's his job. In Rome, the food taster was called a praegustator, which is Latin for "guy who goes first." Emperor Claudius had one, a man named Halotus. Claudius was allegedly poisoned anyway. Great hire. So how did rulers actually try to survive? King Mithridates of Pontus had a plan: drink a little poison every day, so his body gets used to it. Bold. Others trusted a magic stone from an animal's stomach, the bezoar, said to cure any poison. A French surgeon named Ambroise Paré wanted proof. A cook sentenced to death chose poison over the noose, as long as he got the stone afterwards. He got the stone. Seven hours later, they had their answer.
