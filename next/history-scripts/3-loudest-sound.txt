Sailors on a ship near Sumatra grab their heads. Their eardrums have just burst. The sound came from forty miles away. A hundred miles away, in Batavia, a pressure gauge at the gasworks shoots off the scale. Windows burst. Walls crack. Three thousand kilometres away, people in Perth hear it. Nearly five thousand kilometres away, on the island of Rodrigues, they hear it too. So what made it? At 10:02 in the morning on August 27th, 1883, the volcano Krakatoa exploded. It's still the loudest sound known in history. And the pressure wave it sent out didn't stop at the horizon. It travelled around the entire planet more than three times.
