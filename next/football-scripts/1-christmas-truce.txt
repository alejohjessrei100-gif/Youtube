A tin of bully beef rolls across no man's land. Nobody shoots. It's Christmas Day, 1914, and men who tried to kill each other yesterday are kicking a ball about. That part happened. The famous match is another story. The 3–2 to the Germans comes from a tale Robert Graves wrote in 1962. Historians argue over whether any proper match took place. A photo often shared as the truce match is from a year later, in Greece. But Bertie Felstead, who was there, remembered a free-for-all, maybe fifty men a side, until officers ordered them back. He died in 2001, aged 106. The last known survivor.
