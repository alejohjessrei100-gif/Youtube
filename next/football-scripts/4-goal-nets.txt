Before 1891, football goals had no nets. A ball could fly through the posts, hit the crowd, and bounce back out. Was it a goal? Only the referee decided, and the arguments never stopped. Then a Liverpool engineer named John Alexander Brodie came up with a fix in 1889: hang a net behind the posts. Nets reached matches in 1891. Brodie went on to help design the Mersey Tunnel under the river. Of everything he built, he said the football net was the invention he was proudest of.
