# Football scripts (script only, nothing produced)
Eight single-paragraph scripts, 90 to 105 words each: about 25 to 29 s at 3.6 words/s (voice trimmed and sped to the reference rate, see plan/voice_fast.sh). English. Rules followed: present-tense scene hook, no date opener, the answer held until the middle, short sentences, ends on a concrete fact, no moral, no invented dialogue.

## Sources (all read before writing)
- Christmas truce: Wikipedia "Christmas truce" (football section: Times letter of 1 Jan 1915, Graves's 1962 story with the 3–2 score, Brown and Seaton 1984, Dash 2011, Felstead, the 1915 Thessaloniki photo, bully-beef tin at Le Touquet).
- Trautmann: Wikipedia "Bert Trautmann" and "1956 FA Cup final" (75th minute, no substitutes, St George's Hospital said a crick, X-ray three days later, five dislocated vertebrae, second cracked in two, third wedged against it, 20,000 at the 1949 demonstration).
- Brazil yellow: Wikipedia "Brazil national football team" and "Aldyr Schlee".
- Goal nets: Wikipedia "John Alexander Brodie"; a web search of Everton history sites, sportinglandmarks.co.uk and others.
- Baggio: Wikipedia "1994 FIFA World Cup final".
- Zidane: Wikipedia "2006 FIFA World Cup final".
- Football War: Wikipedia "Football War".
- Hand of God: Wikipedia "Argentina v England (1986 FIFA World Cup)".

## Flags (single source or disputed; check before producing)
1. Christmas truce: the bully-beef tin comes from one reported unit (Lancashire Fusiliers); the hook uses it as an illustration. Historians disagree on whether any organised match happened, so the script does not say it did.
2. Trautmann: "years earlier" is 1949 to 1956. The "may have saved his life" line is Wikipedia's wording (the third vertebra "preventing further damage which could have cost Trautmann his life").
3. Brazil yellow: Schlee dying the day before Brazil v Uruguay in London is one Wikipedia sentence.
4. Goal nets: sources disagree on where and when the first match with nets was played (12 January 1891, North v South in Nottingham, per one source; an FA Cup final in March 1891 per another) and on the patent dates, so the script says only "1891" and "1889". "A ball could fly through the posts, hit the crowd and bounce back out" is an illustration of the problem, not a documented incident. Brodie "helped design" the Mersey Tunnel (Wikipedia: "one of the engineers who led the design").
5. Baggio: nothing here is disputed. The injury detail is Wikipedia's ("an injury earlier in the tournament").
6. Zidane: Wikipedia says the fourth official "confirmed the incident" by headset and that FIFA rejected claims he illegally used video. The script does not say what he saw. It also leaves out what Materazzi said (contested; FIFA found no racial slurs).
7. Football War: casualty counts differ widely so none is used. "Twelve thousand forced to flee" is El Salvador's claim, and the script attributes it to them. "About a hundred hours" is the war's nickname (14 July to the night of 18 July).
8. Hand of God: the linesman line is Maradona's own account from the 2019 documentary. Wikipedia counts "five England players" in one place and names four in another, so the script gives sixty yards in ten seconds instead.
