Every time a ship's siren sounds off the cliffs, he drops everything and runs into the sea.
No boat. Just a long wooden pole to keep him afloat, and a mile of open water.
He's the island's mailman.
Out there, a ship's crew throws a biscuit tin full of letters overboard, and he has to reach it through the current.
Some swims last hours.
At night, he swims with a lantern, and villagers light fires on the cliffs so he can find his way back.
So why does anyone do this? Because the island has no harbour and no beach, just cliffs. No ship can land.
Then a trader sees a chance. He makes a rubber stamp with a child's printing set: TIN CAN MAIL.
Collectors as far away as Canada start sending letters just to get it. Cruise ships show up to watch the tins hit the water.
Then, in 1931, a shark kills one of the swimmers.
The government orders canoes instead. The mail keeps coming for decades.
And the island nobody could land a ship on gets a new name: Tin Can Island.
