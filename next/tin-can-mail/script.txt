He swam a mile through sharks to get the mail. This island has no harbour, just cliffs, so ships couldn't land. Instead, a ship sounded its siren and threw a biscuit tin full of letters overboard. Then a swimmer went out after it. Currents. Hours in the water. And sharks. At night, villagers lit fires to guide the swimmers home. In 1931, a shark killed one of the swimmers. Then a trader made a rubber stamp with a child's printing set: Tin Can Mail. Collectors as far away as Canada mailed letters just to get it. Cruise ships came to watch. That's how it became Tin Can Island.
