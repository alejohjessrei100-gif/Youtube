# Vasa short: script v3 (104 words, ~38.5 s at 2.7 words/s)

## Facts and sources
- Vasa, Swedish royal warship, 64 guns, ~700 carved sculptures; sank 10 Aug 1628 on her maiden voyage, ~1,300 m out,
  32 m deep. Before sailing, 30 men ran across the moored ship's deck; she rolled so badly the test was stopped after
  about 3 runs; she sailed anyway. A first gust heeled her, she righted, a second gust put water in through the open
  lower gun ports. ~30 died (estimates 30-50; not in the script).
- Cause: too top-heavy, ~120 t ballast vs more than twice that needed; more ballast would bring the lower gun ports
  closer to the water (shallow hull, no room). Inquiry: nobody punished. ("King demanded an extra gun deck" is a myth:
  NOT used.)
- Raised 1961 after 333 years; ~98 % original (Vasa Museum, Guinness; older accounts say 95 %). Brackish cold Baltic,
  no shipworm.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasa_(ship) · https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/vasa-history/inquest ·
  https://www.irishtimes.com/world/europe/2023/05/16/the-vasa-was-one-of-the-most-powerful-ships-in-the-world-for-about-20-minutes ·
  https://military-history.org/articles/back-to-the-drawing-board-vasa.htm ·
  https://guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/724007-most-intact-sailing-warship-salvaged ·

## Script
Sweden built a warship with sixty-four cannons, and it lost a fight to a breeze.
You see, in sixteen twenty-eight, Sweden's king ordered a ship to terrify Europe.
Before it sailed, thirty sailors ran across the deck. After three runs, it nearly tipped over.
So they stopped the test, and sailed anyway.
One gust tilted it. A second poured the sea through its open gun ports, thirteen hundred metres out.
It was top-heavy, and adding weight below would have dropped the gun ports to the waterline.
Three hundred thirty-three years later, they raised it, ninety-eight percent intact.
A terrible warship. A perfect time capsule.
