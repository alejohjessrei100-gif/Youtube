# 10 ideas: psychology & human behaviour (2026-11-06), fact-checked. Shape: absurd line 1, then "You see,".

1. Lemon-juice robber (Pittsburgh, 1995). "A man robbed two banks with lemon juice on his face, sure the cameras
   couldn't see him." You see: he'd "tested" it with a Polaroid. The case inspired Dunning & Kruger (1999): the skills
   you lack are the ones you'd need to spot it. Visual: lemon macro, a grinning robber on every CCTV screen.
2. Smoke-filled room (Latane & Darley, 1968). "People sat in a room filling with smoke, and kept filling in forms."
   Alone, 75 % reported it; with two calm strangers (actors), 10 %. Visual: smoke pouring from a vent, three calm faces.
3. The door swap (Simons & Levin, 1998). "Half the people giving directions didn't notice the man they were talking to
   turned into a different man." Two workers carry a door between them; the asker swaps behind it.
4. Invisible gorilla (Simons & Chabris, 1999). "About half the people watching a basketball clip missed a gorilla."
   Counting passes uses up the attention you'd need to see it. Visual: a gorilla suit beating its chest mid-court.
5. Hanoi rat bounty (1902). "Paying people to kill rats gave Hanoi more rats." One cent per tail: tailless rats were
   set free to breed, and some people farmed them. Visual: sewers, rats with no tails.
6. Clever Hans (Berlin, 1904-07). "A horse did maths for crowds, and nobody was cheating." He read his questioner's
   head; 89 % right when he could see them, 6 % when he couldn't.
7. Sham knee surgery (Moseley, NEJM 2002, 180 patients). "Surgeons cut into knees, did nothing inside, and patients
   felt just as much better." Expectation is powerful (the trial led to Medicare / VA advisories).
8. Rigged wheel (Tversky & Kahneman, 1974). "A spinning wheel changed what students believed about Africa." Wheel on
   10 -> they guessed 25 % of UN members are African; wheel on 65 -> 45 %. Anchoring.
9. Asch's lines (1950s). "Three out of four people said a short line was long, at least once." The room said so first.
   Hedge: later replications found much less conformity.
10. Doorways (Radvansky, 2011). "Walking through a door makes you forget why you came in." The doorway marks the end of
   one "event" in memory. Very relatable.
