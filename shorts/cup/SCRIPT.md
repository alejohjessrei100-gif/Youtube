# "The Cup That Changes Colour" — script v1 (target 30-40 s)

## Facts (with sources)
- The Lycurgus Cup: a 4th-century AD Roman glass cage cup, British Museum. Green when lit from the front (reflected
  light), red when light passes through it. (Wikipedia, "Lycurgus Cup")
- The effect comes from colloidal particles of a silver-gold alloy in the glass; electron microscopy (Barber &
  Freestone, Archaeometry 32, 1990) found particles of about 50 nm. A human hair is roughly 50,000-100,000 nm wide,
  so "a thousand times thinner than a hair" is conservative. (MMC abstract; Wikipedia)
- Whether the Romans made it on purpose is still debated; the process was not recorded. (Wikipedia; Hackaday)
- Not used (not verified): ownership history, exact ppm of gold and silver.

## Script
This Roman cup is green. Put a candle behind it, and it turns blood red.
No paint. No hidden trick. Just glass.
It's sixteen hundred years old, and for centuries, nobody could explain it.
Then scientists finally looked inside it with an electron microscope.
You see, the glass is sprinkled with gold and silver,
in specks a thousand times thinner than a hair.
Light bouncing off them looks green. Light shining through them looks red.
And nobody knows if the Romans did it on purpose.
So sixteen centuries ago, someone was doing nanotechnology… and might not have even known.
