# 10 "why do we...?" ideas (everyday psychology), 2026-11-06, fact-checked. Shape: absurd line 1, then "You see,".

1. Why rain feels cozy. "Your nose can smell rain better than a shark smells blood." (Hedged: humans detect geosmin, the
   rain-earth smell, at ~5 parts per trillion.) You see: one theory (Appleton's prospect-refuge) says we relax when we
   are sheltered but can watch the danger; steady rain noise also masks sudden sounds. Payoff is part theory: say so.
2. First night in a hotel. "On your first night somewhere new, half your brain refuses to sleep." Tamaki 2016:
   the left hemisphere stays on watch and wakes you faster to odd sounds. Gone by night two. (Hard, strong visual.)
3. Why you can't tickle yourself. "You are the one person on Earth who can't tickle you." Blakemore (UCL): the
   cerebellum predicts your own touch and mutes it; add a robot delay and it starts to tickle.
4. Time slowing in a crisis. "Scientists dropped people off a tower to see if time slows down." Eagleman 2007: they
   couldn't read a fast-flashing number mid-fall, yet remembered the fall as 36 % longer. Memory, not slow-mo.
5. Songs stuck in your head. "The cure for a song stuck in your head is chewing gum." Beaman 2015: gum cut how often
   people "heard" the song by about a third (the jaw jams your inner singing).
6. Music chills. "A song can hit your brain like food." Salimpoor 2011: dopamine is released before AND at the peak.
7. Your recorded voice. "The voice everyone else hears is not the one you hear." Bone conduction adds bass inside your
   skull; recordings only catch the air version.
8. Nails on a chalkboard. "Your ear is shaped to make the worst sound worse." Reuter & Oehler 2011: the ear canal's
   resonance boosts the squeak's frequencies.
9. Cute aggression. "Seeing something cute makes people want to squeeze it, hard." Yale: people shown cute animals
   popped more bubble wrap (~120 vs ~100 bubbles). The brain balances one big emotion with its opposite.
10. Doorways. "Walking through a door makes you forget why you came in." Radvansky 2011: the doorway closes one
   "event" in memory.
Recommended: 2 (first night), 3 (tickle), 1 (rain, hedged).
