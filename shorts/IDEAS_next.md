# 10 ideas for the next short (2026-11-05), fact-checked

1. Vasa (1628). Hook: "The mightiest warship on Earth sank before it left the harbour." Mystery: why? 30 % payoff: in a
   stability test 30 sailors ran across the deck, she nearly tipped, they launched anyway. Payoff: an extra gun deck
   made her top-heavy; one gust, ~1,300 m out. Button: the shipworm-free Baltic kept her >95 % original.
   Frame 0: admiral's face as the deck tilts, macro of a gilded lion figurehead, water pouring into gun ports.
2. Gimli Glider (1983). Hook: "A brand-new jet ran out of fuel at 41,000 feet. Everyone walked off." Mystery: how do you
   forget fuel? Payoff: pounds vs kilograms, under half the fuel (~10,000 kg vs 22,300). The captain was a glider pilot;
   forward slip onto a disused runway turned racetrack. Frame 0: pilot's face, every cockpit screen going black.
3. Tunguska (1908). Hook: "Something flattened 80 million trees, but the trees in the middle stayed standing." Mystery:
   what, and why the middle? Payoff: an airburst 5-10 km up; the blast hit the centre straight down, stripping branches
   ("telegraph-pole forest", Kulik 1927), no crater. Frame 0: explorer among bare poles, radial fallen trunks.
4. Clever Hans (1904-07). Hook: "A horse did maths for crowds, and nobody was cheating." Mystery: how? Payoff: he read the
   questioner's tiny head lift; 89 % right when he could see them, 6 % when he couldn't. Button: Pfungst could not stop
   giving the cue himself. Frame 0: horse mid-tap, chalkboard sum, gasping crowd.
5. Antikythera mechanism. Hook: "Divers pulled a lump of rock from a shipwreck. It was a computer." Mystery: what did it
   compute? Payoff: X-rays (1971, CT 2005) showed ~2,000-year-old bronze gears predicting eclipses (223-month Saros dial)
   and the dates of the Olympic Games. Frame 0: sponge diver's face, green corroded gear macro.
6. Leaning Tower of Pisa. Hook: "The soil that made this tower lean is why it's still standing." Mystery: why hasn't it
   fallen? Payoff (2018 Roma Tre / Bristol study): soft soil keeps it from resonating with quakes; at least 4 strong
   quakes since 1280. Frame 0: tower tilting as the ground shakes, builders recoiling.
7. Great Molasses Flood (Boston 1919). Hook: "The slowest liquid in your kitchen once outran people." Mystery: how?
   Payoff: a burst tank sent a ~25 ft wave at ~35 mph; warm molasses hit freezing air and set like glue (21 dead,
   ~150 hurt). Frame 0: horrified face, a brown wave over cobbles.
8. Prince Rupert's drop (1660s). Hook: "This glass survives a hammer. Snap its tail and it explodes." Mystery: why?
   Payoff: quenching locks the skin in compression (~700 MPa) and the core in tension; a tail crack races ~1.5 km/s.
   Frame 0: hammer bouncing off a glowing glass tadpole, macro.
9. Statue of Liberty. Hook: "The Statue of Liberty is covered in corrosion, and it's protecting her." Mystery: why
   wasn't it scrubbed off? Payoff: shiny copper in 1886, green by 1906; Congress funded repainting, public outcry;
   the patina shields the copper. Frame 0: a gleaming copper-brown Liberty.
10. Eratosthenes (~240 BC). Hook: "A man measured the whole Earth without leaving Egypt." Mystery: how? Payoff: no shadow
   in a Syene well at the solstice, a 7.2 deg shadow in Alexandria = 1/50 of a circle, x 5,000 stadia. Frame 0: face
   lit by sunlight at the bottom of a well.
Recommended: 1 (Vasa), 3 (Tunguska), 2 (Gimli).
